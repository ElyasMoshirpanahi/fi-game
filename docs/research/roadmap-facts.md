# Roadmap fact check: financial_independence_roadmap_clean.html

Date: 2026-11-09

## Method

Every factual statement in `content/financial_independence_roadmap_clean.html` (Farsi, six sections plus the library and reading order) was listed and checked against primary sources only: the data owners (UBS/DMS Yearbook, MSCI), the original papers (Bengen 1994, Cooley/Hubbard/Walz 1998, Barber/Odean, Chague et al.), the fund issuers (Vanguard, iShares), the brokers' own pages (Interactive Brokers, DEGIRO), the regulators (ESMA), Dutch government sites (belastingdienst.nl, rijksoverheid.nl, government.nl, business.gov.nl, hogeraad.nl, eerstekamer.nl) and, for books, publisher pages, author sites and Library of Congress records. Blog summaries were not used as evidence. Where a primary page could not be reached (geo-blocked or bot-blocked), the row says so and names the fallback that was used. Opinions and prescriptions in the HTML were marked as such and not "verified". Two helper agents did the 28 book lookups; everything else was done directly. Dutch 2026 tax figures were checked on the Belastingdienst pages that were live on the date above.

Verdict legend: Confirmed = source says what the HTML says. Nuanced = true in substance but the HTML drops a condition, co-author or caveat that matters. Contradicted = the source says something different. Opinion/prescription = not a checkable fact; recorded, not verified.

## Detail per claim

### 1. 40k EUR at 5 to 8 percent real return is about 2 to 3k EUR a year

HTML: "۴۰ هزار یورو با بازده واقعی ۵–۸٪ ≈ ۲–۳ هزار یورو در سال".

Arithmetic: 40,000 x 0.05 = 2,000; 40,000 x 0.08 = 3,200. So the range is 2.0k to 3.2k, which the HTML rounds to 2 to 3k. Fine.

Is 5 to 8 percent real a defensible historical range for a global equity index? Partly.

- Dimson, Marsh, Staunton, UBS Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2025 (data 1900 to 2024, 35 markets): world equities 5.2 percent a year real, bonds 1.7 percent, bills 0.5 percent. Source: Cambridge Judge Business School summary of the 2025 Yearbook (Dimson's institution) at https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2025/report-stocks-have-far-outperformed-over-the-past-125-years/ . The UBS public summary PDF ( https://www.ubs.com/content/dam/assets/wm/static/cio/documents/giry-2025-summary-public.pdf ) states for the 21st century: "Measured since the start of 2000 ... global equity investors still enjoyed an annualized real return of 3.5% and an equity risk premium relative to bills of 4.3%." The 5.2 percent world figure itself sits in a chart in that PDF and could not be text-extracted; the Cambridge page is the readable primary statement of it. US equities since 1900: 6.6 percent real (UBS summary: nominal 9.7 percent with 2.9 percent inflation).
- MSCI World Index (USD) factsheet, as of 29 July 2022 (the only MSCI factsheet reachable without a login): annualized net return since 29 Dec 2000 was 5.78 percent nominal in USD; 10-year 10.21 percent nominal. Index launched 31 March 1986, earlier data back-tested. MSCI does not publish a real (inflation-adjusted) long-run series in the factsheet. Source: https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/149ed7bc-316e-4b4c-8ea4-43fcb5bd6523 .
- FTSE All-World: no long-run real return factsheet reachable; the Vanguard VWCE page gives fund returns only since 2019. Not used.

Reading: 5 percent real is the long-run global figure. 8 percent real is above anything the long-run global data show; it exceeds even the US record (6.6 percent). The lower bound of the HTML range is defensible; the upper bound is optimistic.

Proposed card wording: "40k EUR at a long-run real return of about 5 percent gives roughly 2k EUR a year; world equities returned 5.2 percent a year after inflation over 1900 to 2024 (DMS/UBS Yearbook), and only 3.5 percent since 2000. Treat anything above 6 percent real as a best case, not a plan."

### 2. Capital of at least 25 times annual expenses

HTML: "سرمایه ≥ ۲۵ برابر هزینه سالانه". This is the 4 percent rule inverted (1/0.04 = 25).

Origin, Bengen, "Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data", Journal of Financial Planning, October 1994 (FPA reprint PDF, text extracted): data from Ibbotson Associates' Stocks, Bonds, Bills and Inflation 1992 Yearbook, US large-cap stocks and intermediate-term US Treasuries, retirements starting each year from 1926, base case 50/50 continually rebalanced. Key sentence: "Assuming a minimum requirement of 30 years of portfolio longevity, a first-year withdrawal of 4 percent, followed by inflation-adjusted withdrawals in subsequent years, should be safe. In no past case has it caused a portfolio to be exhausted before 33 years, and in most cases it will lead to portfolio lives of 50 years or longer." He recommends stock allocations "as close to 75 percent as possible, and in no cases less than 50 percent" and notes he assumes tax-deferred accounts, so no capital-gains taxes.

Trinity study, Cooley, Hubbard, Walz, "Retirement Savings: Choosing a Withdrawal Rate That Is Sustainable", AAII Journal, February 1998, vol. 20 no. 2, pp. 16 to 21 (AAII page fetched): S&P 500 and long-term high-grade corporate bonds, 1926 to 1995, payout periods 15 to 30 years, five stock/bond mixes. Text: "withdrawal rates of 3% and 4% are extremely unlikely to exhaust any portfolio of stocks and bonds during any of the payout periods shown" and, with inflation-adjusted withdrawals, "withdrawal rates of 3% to 4% continue to produce high portfolio success rates for stock-dominated portfolios." The success-rate tables themselves are images on the AAII page and could not be read, so the often-quoted "95 percent for 50/50 over 30 years" is not quoted here. The authors' 1999 follow-up (Financial Counseling and Planning, data through December 1997; a copy of the paper was read, hosted by a third party) summarises: "portfolios of at least 75% stock provide 4% to 5% inflation-adjusted withdrawals." Their 2011 update in the Journal of Financial Planning uses 1926 to 2009 data ( https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/article/journal/APR11-portfolio-success-rates-where-draw-line ).

What this implies for a European investor: both studies use US stock and US bond returns, a 30-year horizon, 50 to 75 percent in stocks, and ignore tax and fees. A global equity investor earned 5.2 percent real since 1900 against 6.6 percent for the US, so the 4 percent figure was derived on the best-performing major market. A horizon longer than 30 years (early retirement) also lowers the safe rate in Bengen's own charts (3 to 3.5 percent lasts 50 years in all periods). Dutch Box 3 tax on the capital itself (see row 11) is an extra drag the studies did not model.

Proposed card wording: "The 25x rule is the 4 percent rule from Bengen (1994) and the Trinity study (1998): 4 percent initial withdrawal, inflation-adjusted, lasted at least 30 years in every US period since 1926 with 50 to 75 percent in stocks. It was built on US returns, a 30-year horizon and no taxes, so a European early retiree should treat 25x as a floor, not a target."

### 12. Tegenbewijsregeling after the Hoge Raad rulings

- 24 December 2021 (Kerstarrest): the Hoge Raad held the 2017 Box 3 system violates the right to property (art. 1 First Protocol) and the ban on discrimination (art. 14 ECHR) and gave redress by taxing only the actual return in that case. Press release: https://www.hogeraad.nl/actueel/nieuwsoverzicht/2021/december/vermogensrendementsheffing-box-3-2017-2018-strijd-eigendomsrecht/ (ECLI:NL:HR:2021:1963).
- 6 June 2024: the Herstelwet and the Overbruggingswet still violate the ECHR "in de gevallen waarin het forfaitaire rendement hoger is dan het werkelijke rendement". The Hoge Raad defined actual return: whole Box 3 estate including bank deposits, no deduction of the heffingsvrij vermogen, nominal (no inflation adjustment), per year only, includes interest, dividend, rent and realised and unrealised value changes, costs not deductible except interest on Box 3 debts. Burden of proof on the taxpayer; no interest paid on the refund except where statutory interest would exceed the tax reduction. Press release: https://www.hogeraad.nl/actueel/nieuwsoverzicht/2024/juni/hoge-raad-box-3-heffing-steeds-discriminerend/ (ECLI:NL:HR:2024:704, 705, 756, 771, 813).
- Wet tegenbewijsregeling box 3: adopted by the Tweede Kamer 12 June 2025 and the Eerste Kamer 8 July 2025, in force 19 July 2025 (rijksoverheid timeline https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/inkomstenbelasting/box-3/tijdlijn-rechtsherstel-box-3 ). ### 16. Tax residency

### 21. Forex, signals, day trading, leverage: almost everyone loses

- ESMA, 27 March 2018 (measures agreed 23 March 2018): national competent authorities' analyses found that "74-89% of retail accounts typically lose money on their investments" in CFDs (CFDs are how retail forex and leveraged index trading are sold in the EU). ESMA imposed leverage limits of 30:1 to 2:1, negative balance protection and a standard risk warning showing each provider's loss percentage. Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors . The formal text is ESMA Decision (EU) 2018/796 (OJ L 136, 1 June 2018); EUR-Lex returned an empty page to the fetcher, so the press release is the cited primary.
- Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, Zhang, "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?" (October 2017 version, complete Taiwan Stock Exchange data 1992 to 2006): "the aggregate performance of day traders net of fees is negative in each of the 15 years that we study"; "more than 75% of all day traders quit within two years"; predictably profitable traders "constitute less than 3% of all day traders on an average day"; an earlier paper by the same group finds less than 1 percent of the day-trading population predictably earns profits. Source PDF: https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf (text extracted).
- Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (University of Sao Paulo working paper 2019-47; SSRN 3423101, revised 2020): of individuals who began day trading Brazilian equity index futures in 2013 to 2015 and persisted for at least 300 days, "97% of them lost money" and only 0.4 percent earned more than a bank teller (US$54 a day) in the 2019 version; the 2020 SSRN abstract gives 1.1 percent earning more than the Brazilian minimum wage. Source: https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/spawpaper/2019wpecon47.htm (SSRN returned 403).

Verdict: Confirmed. "Almost everyone" is exact for persistent day traders (97 percent) and slightly strong for retail CFD accounts (74 to 89 percent). Proposed card wording if the owner wants numbers: "Regulators found 74 to 89 percent of retail CFD/forex accounts lose money (ESMA 2018); 97 percent of Brazilians who day traded for 300+ days lost, and under 3 percent of Taiwanese day traders were predictably profitable."
